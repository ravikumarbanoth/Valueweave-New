# Collection Report: career_paths.csv

Package: Package006_Skills_and_Training
Dataset: `datasets/career_paths.csv`
Collection date: 2026-07-24
Researcher: automated research agent (WebSearch only; WebFetch to .gov.in/.ac.in/.nic.in blocked in this environment)

## Methodology

- Research was conducted exclusively via WebSearch (WebFetch to `.gov.in`, `.ac.in`, and `.nic.in` domains returns HTTP 403 in this environment and was not used). Search-result snippets and PDFs surfaced from government/quasi-government domains (dgt.gov.in, ncvet.gov.in, nqr.gov.in, nsdcindia.org, cbseacademic.nic.in, mnre.gov.in, nabard.org) and Sector Skill Council sites (rasci.in, thsc.in, sscamh.com, ficsi.in, essc-india.org, healthcare-ssc.in, iascsectorskillcouncil.in) were treated as Tier 1/2 evidence; industry blogs and training-provider sites (used mainly for DGCA drone licensing and BPO/cloud career-ladder framing, where no fetchable government source was found) were treated as Tier 4/5 and flagged accordingly in `notes`.
- 15 real career pathways were researched, starting with the brief's own worked example (ITI Electrician -> Automation Consultant, row 1) — this was independently re-verified rather than copied verbatim, and a genuine NSQF-level discrepancy in the underlying DGT documentation was uncovered and documented (see Conflicts below) — plus 14 more spanning manufacturing/trades (welding, CNC machining, fitting, plumbing), green/emerging skills (solar PV, drone piloting), services (retail, hospitality, BPO, IT support, digital marketing), self-employment/craft (tailoring), agri-processing (food processing/FPO), and healthcare support.
- Each pathway's `stage_sequence` cites the specific Qualification Pack (QP), NSQF level, and issuing body where one was found (e.g. RASCI's five-level retail ladder from NSQF 2 to NSQF 7; AMHSSC's Self Employed Tailor QP AMH/Q1947; HSSC's General Duty Assistant QP HSS/Q5101), so each row is independently checkable against the National Qualification Register (nqr.gov.in) or the relevant SSC site.
- `career_path_id` values are freshly generated UUIDv4s. `collection_date` is `2026-07-24` and `verification_status` is `VST-NEEDS_REVIEW` for every row, per instructions.
- The WebSearch tool's per-session query budget was exhausted (200/200) partway through research (after 12 of ~16 planned search rounds), before three planned follow-up lookups could be completed: a specific NASSCOM/IT-ITeS SSC "Team Leader" QP code for BPO, a confirmed list of MeitY-cleared cloud-computing QPs, and further RASCI cross-checks (which turned out not to be needed, as sufficient data had already been found). Rather than fabricate these specific details, the affected cells describe what is and isn't confirmed in `notes`, and the affected rows' `confidence_score` was set at the lower end of the qualitative-but-documented band (55-60) to reflect the gap. No cell in the final dataset uses the bare `PENDING_VERIFICATION` string — every field had at least a partial, honestly-caveated source; where a specific sub-claim (e.g. an exact QP code) could not be confirmed, that gap is explained in `notes` instead, since the surrounding pathway itself was independently corroborated by multiple sources.

## Notable findings

- **The brief's own worked example needed a real correction.** Verifying "ITI Electrician -> Apprenticeship -> Industrial Electrician -> Automation Technician -> PLC Engineer -> Automation Consultant" against the current DGT/NCVET Electrician CTS 2.0 Qualification File shows the trade filed at **NSQF Level 4**, not the Level 5 figure that circulates on many secondary ITI-directory sites (likely a holdover from the pre-CTS-2.0 scheme). The overall stage sequence held up as realistic, but the exact NSQF level required a caveat rather than a blind copy.
- **This same NSQF-level ambiguity recurs across several DGT trades** (Electrician, Welder, Fitter, Plumber): different curriculum revisions (CTS vs. CTS 2.0, 1-year vs. 2-year variants, CITS instructor-level vs. base-trade) carry different NSQF levels in different official documents, and secondary sources often conflate them. Four of the fifteen rows (Electrician, Welder, Fitter, Plumber) carry an explicit `notes` flag on this exact issue rather than picking one figure arbitrarily.
- **Retail is the best-documented pathway in the dataset.** RASCI publishes a fully coherent, explicitly leveled five-stage NSQF ladder (Retail Store Operations Assistant NSQF 2 -> Retail Sales Associate NSQF 3 -> Retail Team Leader NSQF 4.5 -> Deputy Manager NSQF 5 -> Retail Store Manager NSQF 7), each with a named Qualification Pack and stated experience thresholds, and the set is formally NCVET-adopted. This is the strongest single evidentiary chain found in this collection run.
- **Entrepreneurial "exit" stages are systematically the weakest-documented part of almost every pathway.** Becoming a fabrication business owner, solar EPC entrepreneur, boutique owner, drone survey business owner, plumbing contractor, or digital marketing agency owner is a genuinely common, real-world outcome reported across ITI/SSC program materials and industry sources — but with the partial exception of AMHSSC's "Self Employed Tailor" QP (which explicitly names self-employment as its designed outcome) and MNRE's Suryamitra programme (which has a built-in entrepreneurship module), none of these entrepreneurial transitions is itself governed by an NCVET qualification. This is reflected honestly by scoring the entrepreneurial-heavy rows lower and by flagging the specific unsupported stage in `notes` rather than by omitting these very real pathways.
- **Healthcare required special care to avoid overstating a progression.** The General Duty Assistant (GDA) role is a well-documented NSQF Level 4 HSSC qualification, but ANM/GNM nursing qualifications are separate, Indian Nursing Council-regulated formal diploma programmes requiring independent admission — GDA experience does not automatically confer entry or credit toward them. `stage_sequence` and `notes` for that row were deliberately worded to make this distinction explicit rather than implying a direct escalator that doesn't exist.

## Conflicts encountered and how they were resolved

1. **Electrician NSQF level**: DGT's current CTS 2.0 Qualification File names it Level 4; several secondary/ITI-directory sources describe (an older or composite version of) the course as Level 5. Both figures are reported in `notes` with the primary DGT document's figure (Level 4) used in `stage_sequence`.
2. **Welder NSQF level**: the DGT CTS 2.0 Qualification File for Welder (Fabrication & Fitting) is filed as NSQF Level 3; multiple secondary sources describe the trade as Level 4. Both are noted; Level 3 (from the primary document) is used in `stage_sequence`.
3. **Fitter NSQF level**: the current DGT CTS 2.0 file is Level 4, but the separate CITS Fitter *instructor*-training qualification is Level 6, and older sources cite Level 5 for the base trade — these are three distinct things that get conflated in secondary sources. The row's `notes` explicitly separates the base-trade level from the instructor-level qualification to avoid this conflation.
4. **Plumber NSQF level**: sources show Levels 3, 4, and 5 attached to different curriculum durations/versions (1-year CTS vs. 2-year ATS). A range is given in `stage_sequence` with the ambiguity explained in `notes` rather than asserting one figure.
5. **DGCA/eGCA primary sourcing**: the drone-pilot pathway's regulatory facts (Remote Pilot Certificate requirement, eGCA portal, age/education minimums) are consistent across multiple third-party RPTO training-provider sites, but the primary DGCA/eGCA government pages could not be fetched in this environment (403 on .gov.in domains). This is flagged in `data_source`/`notes` as a lower-tier sourcing situation even though the underlying facts are corroborated across independent secondary sources.
6. **BPO "Team Leader" QP**: no specific NASSCOM/IT-ITeS SSC Qualification Pack code for a BPO "Team Leader" role could be confirmed before the WebSearch budget was exhausted for this session. The overall Associate -> Senior Associate -> Team Leader -> Manager ladder is treated as a genuinely well-documented industry-standard progression (Tier 5, qualitative) rather than fabricating a specific QP code, and this is explained in the row's `notes`.
