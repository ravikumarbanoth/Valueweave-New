Each of the three affected rows documents both its individual identity (since each still has distinct target beneficiaries, delivery mechanics and eligibility) and this umbrella restructuring, rather than collapsing them into one row or ignoring the rename.

However, TSSDC's own site returned only thin identity/branding information in available search snippets (no confirmed flagship programme list, no confirmed nodal department, and the site serves over plain HTTP rather than HTTPS, suggesting it may be lightly maintained) — this row therefore carries the lowest confidence score (58) in the dataset and several `PENDING_VERIFICATION` cells, honestly reflecting a real data gap rather than being padded with unconfirmed detail.

## Notable findings

- **"Skill India Programme" is now the umbrella brand for three previously separate central schemes.** A 2025 Cabinet restructuring (PIB-confirmed) combined PMKVY 4.0, the renamed "Pradhan Mantri National Apprenticeship Promotion Scheme (PM-NAPS)" (formerly just NAPS), and the Jan Shikshan Sansthan (JSS) scheme under one composite Central Sector Scheme with a combined ~Rs 8,800 crore outlay through FY2025-26. This is an important rename/restructuring event that would be easy to miss if researching each scheme in isolation — each of the three affected rows documents it explicitly.
- **NATS and NAPS/PM-NAPS are frequently confused but are genuinely distinct schemes** run by different ministries: NATS (National Apprenticeship Training Scheme) is administered by the Board of Apprenticeship Training (BOAT) under the Ministry of Education and targets diploma/degree graduates, while NAPS/PM-NAPS is administered by DGT under MSDE and targets ITI/trade-level apprentices under the Apprentices Act. No evidence of the two schemes merging was found in this session's searches, despite their similar names.
- **Telangana's mandated state skill body is TSSDC, not TASK.** The task brief specifically warned against assuming TASK covers this role, since TASK is already documented in the sibling MSME package as an incubation/employability body. Search confirmed a separate entity, Telangana State Skill Development Corporation (`tssdc.in`, "Telangana Skill Hub"), but it is thinly documented in available sources — a genuine finding rather than a research shortfall to be glossed over, and flagged as needing follow-up verification (including whether TSSDC remains the current active body, given its site still serves over plain HTTP).
- **STRIVE (Skills Strengthening for Industrial Value Enhancement) appears to have concluded.** It was a 5-year World Bank Program-for-Results project with an original closing date of November 2022, extended by 18 months to May 2024. No evidence of a further extension or named successor project was found in this session. It is retained in the dataset as a real, distinct DGT/World Bank skill-quality instrument (per the task's suggestion to include it as an example "additional" scheme), but its current (2026) operational status is flagged unverified in `notes` rather than presented as an active, currently-enrolling programme.
- **SANKALP shows a documented implementation gap.** A February 2026 Parliamentary Public Accounts Committee (PAC) review reportedly found only ~44% of the scheme's budgeted provision was disbursed between 2017-18 and 2023-24, even as a February 2026 World Bank press release suggests continuing/renewed World Bank engagement in the broader skills-institute space. The precise relationship between "SANKALP" as originally structured and this newer World Bank package is unresolved and flagged for follow-up.
- **PM-DAKSH has a documented placement-effectiveness concern.** A 2026 Lok Sabha reply reportedly found fewer than half of PM-DAKSH trainees between 2021 and 2024 were successfully placed in jobs — included in `notes` as relevant context for anyone using this scheme profile, without altering the factual `support_offered_summary`/`eligibility_summary` fields.
- **The MSME Ministry does operate a skill-specific instrument distinct from PMEGP/CGTMSE**: its network of Tool Rooms and MSME Technology Centres (e.g., Central Tool Room & Training Centre, Bhubaneswar) delivers both long-term diploma-level tool-and-die-making training and short-term skill-upgradation courses (CNC, CAD/CAM, 3D printing, robotics) — a genuine skill-training scheme rather than a funding/subsidy instrument, satisfying the task's requirement for an MSME skill-specific row without duplicating PMEGP or CGTMSE.

## Conflicts encountered and how they were resolved

1. **Skill Loan Scheme loan ceiling**: most sources describe the IBA model scheme's ceiling as Rs 5,000–1.5 lakh; one source (BankBazaar) states this has since been increased to Rs 7.5 lakh. Both figures are reported in `support_offered_summary` with the conflict flagged in `notes` as unverified, rather than silently picking one.
2. **NATS monthly stipend range**: sources give inconsistent stipend bands (Rs 3,542–9,000 in one source vs. Rs 6,800–12,300 in another, likely reflecting different qualification tiers or update dates). Both ranges are reported with the discrepancy noted rather than resolved to a single figure.
3. **T-IDEA/APSSDC-style "10 lakh learner" and "20 lakh jobs by 2030" AP figures**: these ambitious targets come from news coverage of a 2026 policy launch rather than a primary APSSDC notification; retained as reported figures with the sourcing caveat made explicit in `notes`.
4. **STRIVE and SANKALP current operational status**: rather than assume these World-Bank-linked schemes are still fully active in 2026 simply because their DGT/MSDE pages exist, both rows explicitly flag the uncertainty (STRIVE's confirmed closing date of May 2024 with no found successor; SANKALP's PAC-flagged implementation delays alongside a newer, not-clearly-linked World Bank press release).
